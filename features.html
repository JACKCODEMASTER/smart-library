Each feature below removes one real barrier standing between a child in Niger and the ability to read — powered by generative AI, machine learning, and the Internet of Things.

Feature 01 · Generative AI

An AI tutor that writes lessons in the child's own language

The tablet's AI tutor generates original stories, vocabulary exercises, and reading passages in each child's native language — Hausa, Zarma, and others spoken across Niger. It introduces new words with supporting images, like the example above, acting as a specialized, always-available teacher.

Why it matters

Most children in Niger have never had access to a personal tutor. Generative AI makes that possible at near-zero marginal cost, in the language the child already speaks.

Feature 02 · Machine Learning

Lessons that adapt the moment a child struggles — or succeeds

Predictive models track every answer: which questions a child got right, how many attempts each one took, and what time of day they focus best. A 90% on a spelling quiz tells the system it's time to raise the difficulty; repeated mistakes trigger extra practice before moving on.

Why it matters

No two children start at the same reading level. Machine learning means a library with 12 tablets can still give each child a personalized path — just like Lexia, at a fraction of the cost.

Feature 03 · Internet of Things

Sensors that keep every library powered, safe, and online

Solar panel and battery sensors report power levels in real time so tablets never die mid-lesson. Cameras and fingerprint locks protect the hardware, and every device syncs attendance and progress data to a central dashboard our team checks daily.

Why it matters

Rural communities rarely have reliable power, internet, or on-site IT support. IoT monitoring lets a small team manage dozens of unmanned libraries remotely — catching problems before a child ever notices.
