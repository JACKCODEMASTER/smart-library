# Design Inspiration — Three Reference Sites

## 1. charity: water — https://www.charitywater.org
**What I like and borrowed:**
- The homepage opens with a single, massive, emotionally direct headline over a full-bleed real photo of the people the mission serves — not an abstract graphic.
- Big, blunt impact numbers shown right on the homepage (e.g., people reached, wells funded) — real counters, not vague claims.
- Every section ends in a clear, singular action ("Ready to take action?") rather than multiple competing CTAs.
- **Borrowed into Smart Library:** The homepage hero pairs two large, animated statistic counters (90% poverty, 93% can't read to grade level) directly beside real photographs of Nigerien children — the same "number + real face" emotional pairing — and nearly every section section ends with one clear CTA button.

## 2. Lexia Learning — https://www.lexialearning.com
**What I like and borrowed:**
- Clean, confident, "science-backed" tone — headlines emphasize credibility ("Science of Reading," "Grounded in research") rather than just emotion.
- Clear separation between *why the method works* (research/technology) and *who it's for* (districts/educators), each getting its own well-organized section.
- Literacy-specific iconography and a professional, education-sector color palette (not overly playful, since the audience is adults making purchasing/partnership decisions).
- **Borrowed into Smart Library:** The dedicated `features.html` page explicitly separates "how the technology works" (Generative AI / Machine Learning / IoT pillars) from "who it serves," and a closing spec table ties each feature to a concrete technology — mirroring Lexia's research-credibility structure, adapted for a hardware+AI nonprofit instead of a SaaS product.

## 3. Pencils of Promise — https://pencilsofpromise.org
**What I like and borrowed:**
- Leads with a stark global-literacy statistic ("250 Million Children Aren't Learning Basic Reading and Writing Skills") immediately under the hero — the problem comes before the pitch.
- Multiple clearly labeled donor/partner pathways (Donate, Fundraise, Give Monthly, Partner with Us) rather than one generic "Contact" link — it segments its audience by how they want to help.
- Community/impact framing throughout, not just product framing.
- **Borrowed into Smart Library:** The home page's "The Problem" section leads with the UNICEF statistic before any solution messaging, and `get-involved.html` segments partners into distinct tracks (Local Government / Donor Organizations / Corporate Data Partners) plus tiered one-click donation amounts, directly modeled on PoP's multi-pathway giving structure.

## What Was *Not* Copied
Layout structure, literal page content, and visual assets are original to Smart Library — no text, images, or code were copied from these sites. Only the *patterns* (stat-forward hero, problem-before-solution ordering, segmented CTAs, credibility-first feature framing) were studied and reinterpreted in our own color palette (solar orange / deep sky blue / sand), typography (Poppins + Nunito Sans), and copy.
